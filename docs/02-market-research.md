# 02 — Market Research

## How real estate uses live video today

Agents already use live and short-form video heavily, and it works: video listings generate roughly **403% more inquiries** than listings without video, and **73% of homeowners** say they're more likely to list with an agent who offers video marketing — yet only around **26% of agents** consistently use video for every listing, leaving a large addressable gap [[Real Estate Video Statistics 2025]](https://worldmetrics.org/real-estate-video-statistics/) [[Real Estate Marketing Statistics 2026]](https://www.reel-e.ai/blog/real-estate-marketing-statistics).

Platform usage among agents for lead generation, per recent surveys: **Facebook 89%**, **Instagram 65%**, **YouTube 32%**, and **TikTok 28%** (roughly doubling year-over-year) [[Real Estate Marketing Statistics 2026]](https://www.reel-e.ai/blog/real-estate-marketing-statistics). Instagram Reels reportedly generate 67% more engagement than static posts on real-estate accounts, and short (<60s) property videos get 2.5x more shares than longer content — meaning the audience is heavily weighted toward exactly the platforms (IG, TikTok) where a native one-click live API is *not* available (see [04](04-technical-feasibility.md)).

Live streaming specifically: live-streamed property tours saw about **150% growth in 2022** and the trend has continued, with live video called out repeatedly as a top real-estate social media tactic for Q&A-style engagement on new listings [[Real Estate Video Statistics 2025]](https://worldmetrics.org/real-estate-video-statistics/).

## Where live streaming happens today, and its ceiling

Virtual open houses today are run through one of three disconnected tool categories:
1. **3D/static tour platforms** (Matterport, Zillow 3D Home/Showcase) — high production value, zero live interactivity; these are pre-recorded scans, not broadcasts [[Matterport vs Zillow Showcase]](https://squarefootphotography.com/virtual-property-tours-matterport-and-zillow-showcase/).
2. **Generic video/conferencing tools repurposed for real estate** (Zoom, Facebook Live, Instagram Live) — genuinely live and interactive, but completely outside any real-estate-specific system; Zillow's own guidance for listing a "live" open house is literally "paste a Zoom or YouTube link" [[CloudPano: Posting Virtual Open Houses]](https://www.cloudpano.com/blog/mls-marketing-tips-posting-virtual-open-houses-to-attract-buyers-llrze).
3. **Open-house lead-capture apps** (Curb Hero, Open Home Pro, Showable, Wave Connect) — solve digital sign-in and automated follow-up for *in-person* opens, not live-streamed ones. Even so, industry data says **most agents capture fewer than 30% of open-house visitors as usable leads**, illustrating how leaky manual capture is even in the physical/simple case [[Showable 2026 guide]](https://showable.co/blog/open-house-sign-in-apps).

No product in the category currently combines *(a)* a live, interactive broadcast, *(b)* real-estate-specific context (property, valuation, viewing request), and *(c)* structured CRM capture of what happens in the stream. That's the gap Lifesycle Live targets.

## Adjacent proof-of-model: live commerce

Outside real estate, live-shopping platforms have already validated "chat during a livestream → structured lead/order" as a business model: **CommentSold** has driven ~$4B in lifetime sales across 7,000+ merchants and multistreams to TikTok, Facebook, Instagram, and the merchant's own site; **Whatnot** and **TalkShopLive** run similar real-time chat-to-purchase flows, with TalkShopLive charging ~10% GMV commission plus a per-product fee [[Live Shopping Platforms 2026]](https://sourceforge.net/software/live-shopping/) [[Bambuser vs CommentSold vs TalkShopLive]](https://www.influencers-time.com/bambuser-vs-commentsold-vs-talkshoplive-for-mid-market-brand/). Industry guidance explicitly flags that **CRM attribution must be planned as part of initial platform setup, not bolted on afterward** — validating Lifesycle Live's premise that the CRM integration is the differentiator, not the video pipe itself.

## Technical patterns behind live-streaming competitors

To ground Lifesycle Live's own pipe/backend choices (see [06-system-architecture.md](06-system-architecture.md) and [11-infrastructure.md](11-infrastructure.md)) in what production live platforms actually run, this section looks at two hard problems specifically — **(a) how each platform minimizes glass-to-glass latency**, and **(b) what backend pattern each uses to ingest and fan out real-time chat/comments at scale** — across the live-commerce platforms already cited above plus the general-purpose live platforms Lifesycle Live restreams to or draws pattern from (Twitch, Zoom, YouTube Live, Facebook Live).

### Latency: the protocol landscape

The industry has converged on a small set of protocols, each with a clear latency/scale trade-off — there is no single "best" choice, only the right one per use case:

| Protocol | Typical glass-to-glass latency | Transport | Where it's used |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WebRTC** | Sub-second, ~150–500ms on a good network | UDP, peer/SFU | Interactive live-commerce (Whatnot-style buy-now chat), video calls, auctions where a host must react to chat in real time [[WebRTC Latency 2026]](https://www.nanocosmos.net/blog/webrtc-latency/) [[Ant Media: Ultra Low Latency Streaming 2026]](https://antmedia.io/ultra-low-latency-video-streaming/) |
| **RTMP / RTMPS** | ~2–5 seconds | TCP | Ingest leg only (broadcaster → platform) for Zoom, Facebook Live, YouTube Live, Twitch — never the viewer-facing leg [[Zoom RTMP streaming]](https://devforum.zoom.us/t/live-streaming-with-rtmp/63328) [[Facebook Live migrates to RTMPS]](https://www.wowza.com/blog/facebook-live-rtmps) |
| **LL-HLS / LHLS (CMAF)** | ~2–4 seconds at CDN scale | HTTPS, CDN-cacheable | Viewer-facing delivery for YouTube Live, Facebook watch pages — chosen because it reuses standard HTTP CDN infrastructure rather than needing UDP-aware edge nodes [[LL-HLS, CMAF, WebRTC comparison — Mux]](https://www.mux.com/articles/low-latency-live-streaming-developers-guide-ll-hls-webrtc-cmaf) [[Cloudinary: LL-HLS vs CMAF vs WebRTC]](https://cloudinary.com/guides/live-streaming-video/low-latency-hls-ll-hls-cmaf-and-webrtc-which-is-best) |
| **HLS (legacy, no LL)** | 8–30 seconds | HTTPS, CDN-cacheable | Fallback for older clients / connected-TV apps where WebRTC playback isn't available [[LL-HLS vs WebRTC 2026]](https://floatleftinteractive.com/guides/low-latency-streaming-protocols-webrtc-vs-ll-hls-vs-cmaf-2026-guide/) |

Key pattern worth calling out: **the ingest leg and the delivery leg are usually different protocols.** Facebook Live, YouTube Live, Zoom, and Twitch all accept broadcaster ingest over RTMP/RTMPS (simple, TCP, widely supported by every encoder including OBS and mobile RTMP libraries), then transcode server-side into CMAF/LL-HLS segments for the many-viewer delivery leg over a CDN — trading a few seconds of extra ingest latency for delivery that scales to millions of concurrent viewers without needing WebRTC's UDP/SFU infrastructure at the edge [[Live Streaming Architecture: Ingest, Transcoding, Delivery]](https://systemdr.systemdrd.com/p/live-streaming-architecture-ingest). Interactive commerce platforms (Whatnot-style) instead push WebRTC end-to-end via an SFU (Selective Forwarding Unit) — the 2026-standard topology for scaling interactive streams, since an SFU forwards packets without the CPU cost of an MCU's server-side compositing — because sub-second latency is the product (a host has to see and react to "sold!" comments instantly) [[SFU vs MCU vs transcoding gateway]](https://antmedia.io/ultra-low-latency-video-streaming/).

**Implication for Lifesycle Live**: this repo's actual constraint isn't latency choice, it's that the mobile RTMP streamer (`app/src/live/rtmpStreamer.ts`) is a stub because Expo Go can't do native RTMP push — see [04-technical-feasibility.md](04-technical-feasibility.md). Once that's built (Expo dev build + native module), the RTMP-ingest-then-CDN-delivery pattern above matches what Lifesycle Live already does structurally (agent's phone → RTMPS → platform adapter in `server/src/adapters/`), so no architecture change is implied — just confirmation that RTMP-out-the-phone is the industry-standard ingest leg, not a compromise. Sub-second WebRTC end-to-end is not warranted here: Lifesycle Live's comments are ingested by *polling* each platform's own comment API (see below), not by relaying the video itself, so shaving the video leg from ~3s to ~300ms would not meaningfully change how fast a lead gets captured.

### Chat/comment ingestion: the backend pattern

For real-time chat/comment fan-out at scale, the pattern reported across Twitch-style system designs and real-time chat backends is consistently a **two-tier split**, not a single database:

- **Hot path (delivery to viewers)**: an in-memory pub/sub layer — Redis Pub/Sub or an equivalent — handles the actual message fan-out over WebSocket connections. Redis is chosen here specifically because in-memory pub/sub is fire-and-forget and low-latency, which is exactly what's needed for "lightweight, ephemeral events like chat" where losing an occasional message under load is an acceptable trade for speed [[Redis Pub/Sub vs Kafka Streams]](https://medium.com/@2017tejasgupta/battle-of-the-streams-redis-pub-sub-vs-kafka-streams-for-real-time-systems-bdb7f1d18ee9).
- **Durable path (events that must not be lost)**: an event log — Kafka or similar — handles anything that needs durability, replay, or downstream processing: stream-started/ended events, viewer-count aggregation, moderation pipelines, analytics. Kafka's distributed-commit-log model is chosen specifically because consumers can replay messages, which a pure pub/sub can't offer [[Kafka vs Redis for real-time systems]](https://risingwave.com/blog/which-is-better-for-your-needs-apache-kafka-or-redis/) [[Designing Twitch in 45 minutes]](https://designgurus.substack.com/p/how-to-design-twitch-in-45-mins).
- Production real-time chat backends commonly combine both: Redis for live delivery/presence, Kafka for queuing spikes, retries, and durability, with a persistent store (Postgres/Mongo) underneath for anything that has to survive past the live session [[Scalable real-time chat backend with Kafka, Redis, WebSockets]](https://medium.com/@sv.sid1033/how-i-built-a-scalable-real-time-chat-backend-with-kafka-redis-websockets-mongodb-8b1076b6d10f).

Live-commerce platforms follow the same shape at the product level even where their internal stack isn't public: CommentSold's own marketing stresses "real-time sync" across simultaneous multi-platform streams (TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, owned site) with orders and inventory kept consistent, which structurally requires the same hot-path/durable-path split even though CommentSold doesn't publish its internal architecture [[CommentSold multistreaming]](https://ideausher.com/blog/live-commerce-app-like-whatnot/). TalkShopLive's public technology footprint (AWS-hosted, Route53 DNS) confirms a standard cloud-CDN deployment rather than anything exotic [[TalkShopLive tech stack — Crunchbase]](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/talkshoplive/technology). Neither CommentSold nor TalkShopLive publish enough detail to confirm their exact message-bus choice, and that gap should be treated as a genuine unknown, not inferred.

**Implication for Lifesycle Live**: `server/src/services/ingestionService.ts` currently does the simplest thing that could work — an in-process `setInterval` poll (6s) per live broadcast that hits each platform's own comment API, with in-memory dedupe and a per-platform cursor, writing straight to Postgres via TypeORM. That's a reasonable MVP: Lifesycle Live's real-time constraint is fundamentally different from Twitch/Whatnot's, because it isn't relaying millions of viewers' own chat messages through its own infra — it's polling a handful of already-hosted platform comment APIs (Facebook, YouTube, Zoom) at a modest cadence for a single agent's broadcast at a time. A Redis-pub/sub hot path would matter once (a) ingestion needs to run across multiple server instances (today it's a single module-level `Map`, explicitly lost on restart, which is the first thing that would need to change), or (b) the app needs push delivery to the dashboard instead of query-cache polling. A Kafka-style durable event log is unlikely to be justified at Lifesycle Live's scale (one agent, one broadcast, a handful of platforms) unless usage grows to many concurrent brokerages — at which point it would slot in as the durable path underneath the existing per-broadcast poll, per the two-tier pattern above. See [06-system-architecture.md](06-system-architecture.md) for how ingestion currently fits alongside the rest of the server, and [11-infrastructure.md](11-infrastructure.md) for deployment/scaling assumptions this would revise.

## Market implication for Lifesycle Live

- Build for the platforms where the audience and the API access line up (Facebook, YouTube, Zoom-sourced restream) as true one-click MVP targets.
- Treat Instagram and TikTok — where agent adoption is growing fastest but API access is weakest — as "assisted" integrations (capture chat/comments and drive CRM automation around a stream the agent starts natively in-app), not blockers to launch.
- Position against the status quo of "paste a Zoom link into Zillow" and disconnected open-house sign-in apps, not against Matterport/Zillow (which are non-live and complementary, not competitive).
